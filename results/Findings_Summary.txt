# Findings & Summary — LoanTap Personal Loan Credit Risk

## 1. Business Objective

The objective of this case study was to build a credit-risk model for LoanTap's Personal Loan product that predicts whether an applicant is likely to be **Fully Paid** or **Charged Off**.

The key business challenge is the trade-off between:

* **False Negative:** A genuine defaulter is approved → potential NPA and financial loss.
* **False Positive:** A good borrower is rejected → lost customer and revenue.

Therefore, **default detection is more important than simply maximizing accuracy**. 

---

## 2. Dataset & Initial Findings

The dataset contains **396,030 loan records and 27 original features**.

The target variable is highly imbalanced:

* **80.4% Fully Paid**
* **19.6% Charged Off**

This approximately 4:1 imbalance makes accuracy an unreliable measure of model performance. A model could achieve around 80% accuracy simply by predicting most applicants as Fully Paid while detecting very few actual defaulters. 

### Important EDA findings

* Grades **B and C** make up a large portion of the portfolio.
* **RENT** and **MORTGAGE** are the dominant home-ownership categories.
* **Debt consolidation** is by far the most common loan purpose.
* **Small-business loans have the highest observed default rate** among purposes.
* Employment length shows only a relatively weak relationship with default.
* Credit utilization and credit history show more meaningful differences between Fully Paid and Charged Off borrowers.   

---

## 3. Data Preparation

The dataset was prepared through:

* Removal of high-cardinality fields such as `address`, `emp_title`, and `title`.
* Missing-value treatment using median and group-based imputation.
* Conversion of `emp_length` into an ordinal numerical feature.
* Creation of binary indicators for public records, mortgage accounts and bankruptcies.
* Creation of `credit_age_years`.
* Conversion of loan `term` into numerical months.
* IQR-based outlier capping.
* One-hot encoding of categorical variables.
* Standardization of numerical features.

The final modelling dataset contained **32 predictors plus the target variable**. 

---

## 4. Baseline Model — The Accuracy Trap

The initial Logistic Regression model produced:

| Metric                |   Baseline |
| --------------------- | ---------: |
| Accuracy              | **80.58%** |
| ROC-AUC               |  **0.711** |
| Charged Off Precision |   **0.53** |
| Charged Off Recall    |   **0.08** |
| Charged Off F1        |   **0.14** |

The confusion matrix shows that the model correctly identified only **1,914 of 23,302 actual defaulters**, while **21,388 defaulters were incorrectly classified as Fully Paid**. 

### Key Finding

**The 80.6% accuracy was misleading.**

The model was very good at identifying the majority class but extremely poor at identifying the minority class that matters most from a credit-risk perspective.

This established that **class imbalance needed to be addressed before considering the model suitable for underwriting**.

---

## 5. SMOTE — Major Improvement in Default Detection

SMOTE was applied only to the training data while keeping the test set in its original real-world distribution.

The results changed substantially:

| Metric                | Baseline | Logistic Regression + SMOTE |
| --------------------- | -------: | --------------------------: |
| Accuracy              |     ~80% |                    **~66%** |
| ROC-AUC               |    ~0.71 |                   **~0.71** |
| Charged Off Precision |     ~52% |                    **~32%** |
| Charged Off Recall    |      ~8% |                    **~64%** |
| Charged Off F1        |    ~0.14 |                   **~0.42** |

The most important improvement was **Charged Off recall increasing from approximately 8% to 64%** — an approximately **8× improvement in default detection**. 

### Business Interpretation

The reduction in accuracy is not necessarily a negative outcome.

The SMOTE model deliberately identifies more risky borrowers. This increases false positives, but it also prevents many more genuine defaulters from being treated as safe borrowers.

For LoanTap, **missing a defaulter can be substantially more expensive than manually reviewing or rejecting a borderline good borrower**.

---

## 6. Threshold Analysis

The probability threshold creates a direct precision-recall trade-off:

| Threshold | Precision |    Recall |        F1 | Interpretation                        |
| --------: | --------: | --------: | --------: | ------------------------------------- |
|       0.2 |     ~0.21 |     ~0.99 |     ~0.34 | Extremely aggressive                  |
|       0.3 |     ~0.23 |     ~0.93 |     ~0.37 | Very high recall                      |
|       0.4 |     ~0.27 |     ~0.81 |     ~0.40 | High recall                           |
|   **0.5** | **~0.32** | **~0.64** | **~0.42** | **Best F1 / balanced starting point** |
|       0.6 |     ~0.37 |     ~0.44 |     ~0.40 | More conservative                     |
|       0.7 |     ~0.43 |     ~0.26 |     ~0.32 | Many defaulters missed                |
|       0.8 |     ~0.50 |     ~0.10 |     ~0.17 | Very conservative                     |

At the **0.5 threshold**, the model provides the highest F1 score while retaining approximately 64% recall. 

However, **0.5 should not automatically be considered the final business threshold**. The optimal threshold should depend on LoanTap's actual financial cost of:

`Cost of missed defaulter vs. Cost of rejecting a good borrower`

---

## 7. Model Stability

A **5-fold stratified cross-validation** was performed with SMOTE applied inside each fold to avoid data leakage.

The fold-level results were highly consistent:

* ROC-AUC: approximately **0.707–0.711**
* Recall: approximately **0.632–0.644**
* Precision: approximately **0.318–0.320**
* F1: approximately **0.424–0.428**
* PR-AUC: approximately **0.364–0.369**
* Accuracy: approximately **0.660–0.663**

This consistency indicates that the model's performance is reasonably stable across the folds. 

For this imbalanced problem, **PR-AUC is particularly useful because it focuses on the minority class rather than being influenced heavily by the large number of true negatives**. 

---

## 8. Most Important Risk Factors

The Logistic Regression coefficients identified several meaningful signals.

### Features associated with higher default risk

1. **`sub_grade`** — strongest signal in the model
2. **`dti`** — higher debt burden relative to income
3. **`revol_util`** — higher credit utilization
4. **Public-record / bankruptcy indicators**
5. **Longer loan term**

### Features associated with lower default risk

1. **`annual_inc`** — higher income
2. **`credit_age_years`** — longer credit history
3. **`mort_acc_flag`** — presence of mortgage history

These relationships provide interpretable signals that can support underwriting decisions beyond the model's predictions. 

---

# 9. Business Recommendations

### 1. Do not optimize for accuracy

Accuracy should not be the primary metric because of the 80:20 class imbalance.

**Charged Off recall, precision, F1 and PR-AUC should receive greater attention.**

### 2. Use threshold calibration

The decision threshold should be chosen using LoanTap's actual financial cost structure.

* If NPA losses are the dominant concern → lower threshold and prioritize recall.
* If customer acquisition is more important → higher threshold and prioritize precision.
* Use financial outcome data and A/B testing to determine the final operating point. 

### 3. Introduce manual review for borderline applicants

The model should act as a **first-stage underwriting screen**, rather than the sole decision-maker.

Borderline applications can be routed for additional verification or manual review. 

### 4. Strengthen checks around high-risk signals

LoanTap should consider:

* Stricter checks for high `dti`.
* Additional review for high `revol_util`.
* Enhanced due diligence for public-record or bankruptcy indicators.
* Lower loan amounts or shorter terms for borrowers with very short credit histories.
* Additional underwriting criteria for small-business loan purposes.
* Income-to-installment affordability checks. 

### 5. Improve the feature set

The stable ROC-AUC of approximately 0.71 before and after SMOTE indicates that SMOTE improved the model's operating point but **did not materially improve its underlying discrimination ability**.

Future versions should incorporate richer information such as:

* Credit bureau information
* Detailed repayment history
* Better income verification
* Additional borrower-level financial information

These could help improve the model's ability to distinguish between good and risky borrowers. 

---

# 10. Final Conclusion

The central finding of this case study is:

> **A model with higher accuracy is not necessarily a better credit-risk model.**

The baseline Logistic Regression achieved approximately **80% accuracy but detected only 8% of actual defaulters**.

After addressing class imbalance with SMOTE, Charged Off recall increased to approximately **64%**, while accuracy decreased to approximately **66%** and ROC-AUC remained around **0.71**.

Therefore, the **Logistic Regression + SMOTE model is the preferred model for this case study** because it is substantially better aligned with LoanTap's primary business objective: **identifying potential defaulters and reducing NPA risk**. 

However, the model should **not be treated as a final autonomous lending decision system**. Its effectiveness depends heavily on the classification threshold and the relative financial costs of false negatives and false positives.

The recommended approach is to use the model as a **first-tier underwriting screen**, calibrate the threshold using actual financial outcomes, manually review borderline cases, enrich the feature set, and periodically retrain the model as new repayment data becomes available. 

### One-line project takeaway

**SMOTE transformed Logistic Regression from an accuracy-focused model that missed most defaulters into a more business-relevant risk-screening model that detects ~64% of defaulters, while highlighting the need for threshold optimization and better credit-risk features.**
